# Governance

MotionBench follows an Apache-style meritocratic model.

## Roles

| Role | Who | Can |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Contributor** | Anyone who files an issue, submits a run, writes docs, reviews or sends a PR | Participate in every discussion |
| **Committer** | Invited after sustained contributions | Merge PRs, cast binding votes on code and tasks |
| **PMC member** | Invited from committers | Binding votes on releases, RFCs, new committers, governance |
| **Steering group** | 5 seats: 2 maintainers, 1 coach/educator, 1 student representative (18+ or with guardian consent), 1 researcher or industry representative | Sets priorities, resolves deadlocks, owns the code of conduct |

Contributions of every kind count towards committership: code, task design,
running data-collection sessions, reviewing video, answering questions.

## Decisions

* **Lazy consensus** for most changes: a proposal stands unless someone objects
  within 72 hours (7 days for RFCs' final call).
* **Votes**: +1 / 0 / −1. A −1 on code must come with a technical reason and an
  alternative. Releases and RFCs need at least three binding +1 and more +1 than −1.
* All decisions happen in public (GitHub, Discussions). Private channels are only
  for code-of-conduct and security matters.

## What needs an RFC

New or changed tasks, trace or report schema changes, scoring rules or weights,
leaderboard tiers and verification rules, governance changes. See [rfcs/](rfcs/).

## Independence

No single company may hold more than two steering seats. Sponsors fund work;
they do not get votes, leaderboard placement or early access to hidden seeds.
